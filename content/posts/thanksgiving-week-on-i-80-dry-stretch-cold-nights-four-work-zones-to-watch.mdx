Interstate 80 across Nebraska gets a dry run-up to Thanksgiving: no warnings or advisories are active on the corridor as of Sunday morning, Nov. 22 — the one exception is a Special Weather Statement for patchy freezing fog at the Wyoming end — and National Weather Service forecasts for Omaha, central Nebraska, the Panhandle approach and the Wyoming line all show dry pavement weather — sun, clouds, highs in the upper 30s to mid-40s — straight through Thanksgiving Day.

The catch is the cold. Overnight lows sit in the teens and 20s every night this week, and the Cheyenne office's fog statement warns of visibility as low as one-quarter mile with possible light icing on elevated surfaces through Sunday morning. Add the four long-term work zones drivers will roll through between Omaha (mile 454) and Kimball (mile 22), and the holiday drive is less about storms than about frost, fog and narrowed lanes.

## What we know

- **The forecast:** Sunday is sunny and 41-44 degrees across most of the corridor, with Monday and Tuesday cooler and cloudier — highs of 38-40 in the east, low 40s farther west, per National Weather Service forecasts for the [Omaha](https://www.weather.gov/oax/), Hastings, North Platte and Cheyenne forecast areas. Wednesday rebounds to mostly sunny, and Thanksgiving Day itself looks sunny to mostly sunny, 42-43 degrees in the Omaha and [Kearney](/cities/kearney)-area zones, warming toward 50 by Friday and Saturday.
- **The cold nights:** Lows run 19-26 degrees Sunday through Wednesday nights corridor-wide. With clear stretches overnight, expect frost on windshields and the usual slick spots on bridges and overpasses before sunrise — standard late-November driving, not a storm, but the reason to build in warm-up time on Thanksgiving morning.
- **Fog at the west end:** The NWS Cheyenne Special Weather Statement issued 1:45 a.m. MST Sunday covers Kimball and Banner counties — the I-80 miles nearest the Wyoming line — with patchy freezing fog, visibility as low as one-quarter mile and possible light icing on elevated surfaces through Sunday morning. Freezing fog is the one condition that can ice a road fast with no warning; if headlights start haloing west of [Sidney](/cities/sidney), slow down rather than pushing to the state line.
- **No warnings or advisories:** The fog statement is the only active alert on the corridor as of Sunday morning; no watches, warnings or advisories are active at Omaha, Lincoln, Kearney, North Platte or Sidney. That can change — November is the month it changes — so treat this as a snapshot, not a promise.
- **Who confirmed it:** The [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/oax/) forecast offices serving each end of the corridor, via point forecasts and the active-alerts API pulled Sunday morning, Nov. 22, consistent with the [Omaha Thanksgiving-week outlook from 3 News Now](https://www.3newsnow.com/weather/pattern-change-to-bring-cooler-and-dry-weather-to-omaha-for-thanksgiving-week). Work-zone details below are from [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) listings checked Sunday morning and Nebraska Department of Transportation releases.

## Four work zones on the holiday route

These are long-term projects, not new closures — but for anyone driving the full corridor for the holiday, they are the four places the road itself narrows:

1. **Cozad to Darr, mile markers 219-231.** Lane closures in both directions with an 11-foot width limit and a 65 mph speed limit, according to Nebraska 511. NDOT's Nov. 17 update says construction continues on this Dawson County stretch between Exit 222 (Cozad) and Exit 231 (Darr). This is the same [head-to-head farm-country segment east of Lexington](/news/lexington-at-mile-237-head-to-head-traffic-in-farm-country) — freight-heavy and unforgiving of inattention.
2. **Ogallala, mile markers 124-133.** Traffic runs in a two-lane, two-way pattern on the eastbound lanes while westbound lanes are rebuilt, with an 11-foot width restriction and 65 mph limit near Exit 126 (Ogallala/Grant). Nebraska 511 lists project completion as expected this month — see our [Ogallala mile-126 guide](/news/ogallala-at-mile-126-lake-gateway-in-an-i-80-work-zone) for the lay of the land.
3. **Lincoln widening, mile markers 382-395.** I-80 is being widened to six lanes between Milford (Exit 382) and West O Street near [Lincoln](/cities/lincoln), with changing traffic patterns, a 12-foot width limit and a 55 mph speed limit. This is the slowest posted work zone on the corridor — plan on it, especially with holiday traffic bunching near the city.
4. **I-180 interchange, mile markers 400-401.** Bridge replacement work pinches I-80 to two lanes each way with a 12-foot width limit, and the interchange ramps carry recurring weekend closures (noon Saturday to midnight Sunday). The project runs into late 2027. If your holiday route cuts through Lincoln on a weekend, check the 511 map for the ramp detours before you commit to Exit 401.

## Why it matters

Thanksgiving week is the year's biggest road-travel week, and the two heaviest driving days — Wednesday outbound and Sunday inbound — both fall inside this forecast window. Dry roads remove the biggest risk, but the remaining risks are the quiet ones: dark, frosty starts with sun glare on east-west pavement, fog near the Wyoming line, and four narrowed zones where an 11-foot lane leaves no margin for drifting. High-profile vehicles should note the corridor stays breezy even without advisories; gusts were the backdrop for last week's [eastbound closure near Overton](/news/i-80-eastbound-closed-near-overton-after-morning-crash).
