Patchy dense freezing fog is sitting over the Wyoming end of Interstate 80 in Nebraska on Monday morning, cutting visibility to one-quarter mile or less in spots in Kimball County, according to the National Weather Service in Cheyenne.

The Weather Service issued a Special Weather Statement at 3:37 a.m. MST Monday, Nov. 23, covering Kimball and Banner counties among other High Plains counties. Webcams and surface observations show the fog developing early Monday, expected to persist and expand through the morning before dissipating in the mid to late morning hours. The statement expires at 10 a.m. MST (11 a.m. CST).

## What we know

- **Where:** The statement covers Kimball and Banner counties plus Scotts Bluff County in Nebraska and adjacent Wyoming counties. On I-80 that means the Kimball County miles near the Wyoming line — the [Kimball](/cities/kimball) interchanges at Exit 20 (Neb. 71, Kimball West, mile 20.7) and Exit 22 (Kimball East, mile 22.7). Sidney and the rest of Cheyenne County are not listed in the statement.
- **When:** In effect Monday morning, Nov. 23, from the 3:37 a.m. MST issuance through mid to late morning. The alert itself expires at 10 a.m. MST.
- **What:** Patchy dense freezing fog with visibility as low as one-quarter mile or less, plus locally slick or icy conditions where the fog freezes on pavement, bridges and overpasses.
- **Who confirmed it:** The [National Weather Service in Cheyenne](https://www.weather.gov/cys/), via its active-alerts feed pulled Monday morning. No other watches, warnings or advisories are active on the Nebraska I-80 corridor Monday morning.
- **Status:** Active now. As of 6 a.m. CST Monday, Nov. 23, 2026, the statement remains in effect; the Weather Service asks travelers to prepare for sudden drops in visibility and to allow extra time.

## Why it matters

Kimball County holds the westernmost Nebraska miles of I-80, the stretch where Thanksgiving-week drivers cross the state line — and freezing fog is the quietest hazard on the corridor. Unlike snow, it can ice a bridge with no precipitation and no warning. Headlights that start haloing near the Wyoming line are the signal to slow down, add following distance and avoid passing — the statement window covers the full Monday morning commute and the early holiday-travel window. Our [Thanksgiving-week corridor outlook](/news/thanksgiving-week-on-i-80-dry-stretch-cold-nights-four-work-zones-to-watch) called for dry roads statewide; this fog is the exception at the west end.

## What happens next

Recheck the [NWS Cheyenne office](https://www.weather.gov/cys/) and the live [Nebraska 511 map](https://511.nebraska.gov/) before rolling west of the state line — conditions can lift in one mile and close in again in the next. If the fog thickens into a Dense Fog Advisory or a crash closes lanes, we will update this story.
