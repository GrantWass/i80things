Lexington anchors the farm stretch between Kearney and North Platte — and right now that stretch is a work zone in both directions. Long-term construction runs from Cozad to Darr on the west side of town and milling work runs east toward Kearney, with head-to-head traffic, narrowed lanes and a 65-mph limit.

For drivers, Lexington is the midpoint service stop on the Kearney–North Platte run. Take it.

## The exits

**Mile 237 — Lexington.** The main approach into Dawson County's hub city, with fuel, food and lodging near the interchange. The [City of Lexington](https://cityoflex.com/) operates from 406 E. 7th St.; its calendar lists a city council meeting November 10, Veterans Day activities at the military vehicle museum November 11 and a Turkey Trot 5K on November 21.

**Mile 248 — east Lexington.** The eastern access point, closest to the milling work zone that runs from mile 247 toward Kearney.

## The work zones

According to Nebraska 511, construction brackets Lexington on both sides:

- **West: Cozad to Darr (miles 219–232).** Long-term road construction with head-to-head traffic, an 11-foot width limit and a 65-mph limit. NDOT lists new concrete pavement, grading, culverts, bridge replacement and lighting among the work items. A separate listing notes head-to-head traffic specifically between miles 229 and 232.
- **East: miles 247–256.** Milling and road-marking work with intermittent lane closures, plus a westbound left-lane blockage reported between miles 253 and 265.

That means narrowed, shifting lanes for roughly 35 miles around town. Merge early, hold the reduced speed and do not crowd wide farm equipment or work trucks. Check the [current 511 listings](https://511.nebraska.gov/) — late-season striping and milling can change the lane pattern day to day.

## Why Lexington matters on the corridor

- **Farm country.** Lexington sits in the irrigated Platte River valley — corn, cattle and the meatpacking and ag-services economy around them. Grain trucks and equipment use the local roads around both exits during planting and harvest; November is the tail of harvest, so expect slow-moving vehicles on the approaches.
- **Between the hubs.** Kearney is about 35 miles east and North Platte about 60 miles west. Lexington's fuel-and-food cluster is decent, but services thin out quickly west of Darr. If the forecast is deteriorating, this is a sensible place to stop rather than push into the wind toward North Platte.
- **The river road.** US-30 parallels I-80 through here along the historic route that predates the interstate — a useful alternate for local hops, not a substitute at highway speed.
- **Weather split.** The [NWS Hastings](https://www.weather.gov/gid/) office covers this stretch from the east. Valley fog and the season's first ice are the November hazards; check the forecast before you drive.

## What to check before driving

1. [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) for the Cozad–Darr head-to-head zone and the milling zone east of town.
2. The [NWS Hastings](https://www.weather.gov/gid/) forecast and any fog or winter advisory.
3. Fuel in Lexington or Cozad — do not assume the smaller exits between here and North Platte are full-service.
4. The [city calendar](https://cityoflex.com/calendar/) if you are stopping — the Veterans Day museum events and the November 21 Turkey Trot add local traffic.
