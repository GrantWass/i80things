Nebraska State Patrol troopers are on stepped-up Thanksgiving patrols across Interstate 80 from Omaha (mile 454) to Kimball (mile 22) this week, working the national Click It or Ticket seat-belt campaign from Monday, Nov. 23 through Sunday, Nov. 29, according to a [Nov. 24 release from the Nebraska State Patrol](https://statepatrol.nebraska.gov/troopers-ready-patrol-busy-thanksgiving-travel).

Col. Bryan Waugh, the patrol superintendent, said troopers and dispatchers will be working around the clock during what AAA projects as a near-record Thanksgiving: more than 80 million Americans traveling 50-plus miles from home, including nearly 73 million going by car.

## What we know

- **What:** Extra NSP staffing for Thanksgiving travel, tied to the Click It or Ticket campaign Nov. 23-29. The effort is grant-funded through the Nebraska Department of Transportation Highway Safety Office, NSP said.
- **Where:** Statewide, including the full I-80 corridor. The York-area Rural Impaired Driving Enforcement and Reduction (RIDER) task force is running its own holiday push in York and Saline counties — the I-80 miles around Exit 353 (York) and the Highway 81 junction.
- **When:** The Click It or Ticket mobilization runs Monday, Nov. 23 through Sunday, Nov. 29. The RIDER Buzzed Driving is Drunk Driving patrols run through Nov. 29, [KLKN Channel 8 reported](https://www.klkntv.com/rider-task-force-joins-buzzed-driving-is-drunk-driving-campaign/).
- **Who confirmed it:** The Nebraska State Patrol, in its Nov. 24 release quoting Col. Waugh, and follow-up local reporting from KLKN. The RIDER task force — NSP plus the York and Saline county sheriff's offices and York and Crete police — was announced Nov. 19 in [a separate NSP release](https://statepatrol.nebraska.gov/nsp-local-agencies-launch-rider-task-force).
- **Status:** Active now. No weather warnings or advisories were active on the Nebraska I-80 corridor when checked Wednesday morning, Nov. 25 — zero active alerts at Omaha, Lincoln, Kearney, North Platte and Kimball corridor points in the National Weather Service alerts API — and Nebraska 511 showed only the standing long-term construction zones in the Nov. 25 pre-travel sweep.

## Why it matters

Thanksgiving week is the heaviest driving week of the year, and on I-80 the risk stacks up in familiar places: the Lincoln widening (mile markers 382-395, 55 mph), the I-180 interchange work at mile 400-401, and the narrowed head-to-head stretches near Cozad-Darr (mile markers 219-231) and Ogallala (mile markers 124-133) detailed in our [Thanksgiving-week corridor outlook](/news/thanksgiving-week-on-i-80-dry-stretch-cold-nights-four-work-zones-to-watch).

Seat belts are the focus because they are the cheapest life-saver in those zones. A lane departure in an 11-foot work-zone lane at highway speed is the crash troopers work most on holiday weeks — and the one a belt most often turns from fatal to survivable. The RIDER angle matters for the same reason: the five agencies formed the unit after counting more than 2,400 crashes, 20 deaths and 100-plus serious injuries in York and Saline counties over five years, as we reported in [Five agencies launch RIDER impaired-driving task force around York](/news/five-agencies-launch-rider-impaired-driving-task-force-around-york).

NSP's holiday checklist is short: drive sober, buckle up, watch speed, put the phone down, and move over for vehicles on the shoulder. Motorists who need help can call the NSP Highway Helpline at *55.

## What happens next

The campaigns end Sunday, Nov. 29 — the Sunday return crush that typically rivals Thanksgiving Eve outbound traffic. NSP usually releases preliminary holiday-weekend enforcement totals the following week; we will add them to this story when the patrol publishes them.
