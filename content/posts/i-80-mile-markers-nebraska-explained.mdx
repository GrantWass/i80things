If you have ever heard a scanner call or a road report place a crash "at mile 272" and had no idea whether that was Kearney or Kimball, this page is for you.

Nebraska's Interstate 80 runs about 454 miles, from the Wyoming state line in Kimball County to the Missouri River in Omaha. The mile markers start at zero at the western border and count **up** as you drive east. Exit numbers are the same as mile markers. That is the whole system, and it is the most useful thing to know about the corridor.

## How the numbering works

- **Mile 0** is the Wyoming line west of Kimball.
- **Mile 454** is the Missouri River crossing between Omaha and Council Bluffs, Iowa.
- **[Exit 272](/exits/272)** is the Kearney interchange at mile 272.
- Driving east, the numbers go up. Driving west, they go down.
- If someone says "I-80 eastbound near mile 312," they mean just east of Grand Island.

This matters because Nebraska is long and the towns are not evenly spaced. A 50-mile gap between exits is normal west of Kearney. A 3-mile gap is normal in Omaha.

Hastings is not on the interstate. It sits about 16 miles south of [Exit 312](/exits/312) via US-281, and it is still one of the corridor's biggest media and medical markets.

## The service gaps

This is the part that catches drivers out. East of Lincoln, you are rarely more than 10 minutes from fuel, food or a hospital. West of Kearney, the spacing changes:

- **Kearney to North Platte:** about 95 miles, with Lexington and Gothenburg in between.
- **North Platte to Ogallala:** about 51 miles, with Paxton in between.
- **Ogallala to Sidney:** about 67 miles, with Chappell in between.
- **Sidney to Kimball:** about 37 miles, and then the Wyoming line.

If you are low on fuel west of North Platte, stop. The next exit may be a grain elevator, not a gas station. In winter, the gap is also a gap in shelter.

## How to give a location

The most useful thing you can do when reporting a hazard is give the mile marker and direction:

> "I-80 westbound, just past mile 272, right lane blocked."

That is more precise than a town name and it works for dispatchers, tow operators and reporters. If you only know the exit, use the exit number — it is the same number.

## Why the markers are worth knowing

Mile markers are how Nebraska 511, the State Patrol, NDOT and every county sheriff describe the corridor. They are also how we organize coverage.
