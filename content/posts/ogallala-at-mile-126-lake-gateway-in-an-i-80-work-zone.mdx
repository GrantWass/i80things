Ogallala is where the interstate meets the lake crowd. The town of about 4,800 is the gateway to Lake McConaughy, Nebraska's largest reservoir, and it sits in the middle of a long-term I-80 work zone that narrows the highway to one lane in each direction.

For drivers, Ogallala is a fuel-and-check stop: fill the tank, check [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) for the work zone, and decide whether the lake detour is worth the extra time.

## The exits

**Mile 126 — Ogallala and Grant.** The western exit, serving the town itself and the Highway 61 approach to Lake McConaughy to the northeast. According to Nebraska 511, westbound traffic uses temporary ramps here to reach the Exit 126 interchange during phased construction.

**Mile 133 — Roscoe.** The eastern exit, five miles east of town. Both directions show active construction between mile 124 and this exit, according to Nebraska 511.

## The work zone

According to Nebraska 511, the long-term project between mile 124 and Exit 133 shifts traffic into a two-lane, two-way configuration on the eastbound lanes — one lane in each direction — while crews work on the westbound side. The details drivers need:

- **Speed limit 65 mph** through the zone.
- **Width limit 11 feet**, which matters for wide loads and towing combinations.
- **Left-lane closures** in both directions at times, with guardrail, lighting and signage work listed as active.

NDOT had expected the two-way configuration to run into October 2026 with project completion in November 2026, according to the 511 listings. Those dates were estimates. Check the [current 511 listing](https://511.nebraska.gov/) before you drive it — late-season work can extend with weather.

## Why Ogallala matters on the corridor

- **Lake McConaughy.** At 30,000 surface acres, it is Nebraska's largest reservoir, about eight miles northeast of town via Highway 61, according to Nebraska Game and Parks. The white-sand beaches draw summer boat traffic that changes the pace at Exit 126.
- **Check the lake before you tow.** Game and Parks maintains current water-level, ramp and access notices. Low-water years can close some public ramps and push boaters to private launches, so check before you drive out.
- **The I-76 junction.** West of Ogallala, I-80 meets I-76 toward Denver, funneling Colorado traffic through Keith County. Expect out-of-state plates and merging trucks west of Exit 126.
- **Cowboy history.** The [City of Ogallala](https://www.ogallala-ne.gov/) points visitors to Front Street, Boot Hill and the Mansion on the Hill museum — a compact downtown stop a short drive from the interstate.

## What to check before driving

1. [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) for the mile 124–133 work zone and anything new west toward Paxton.
2. The [NWS North Platte](https://www.weather.gov/lbf/) forecast — this stretch gets wind-driven winter weather off the high plains.
3. Fuel. Ogallala has full services; the stops west toward Sidney are small. North Platte, with its hospital and repair shops, is about 51 miles east.
4. Lake conditions at [Game and Parks](https://outdoornebraska.gov/location/lake-mcconaughy/) if you are towing a boat or booking a campsite — off-season camping is first-come, first-served.
