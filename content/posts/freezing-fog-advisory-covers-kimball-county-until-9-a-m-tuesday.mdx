Freezing fog is back on the Wyoming end of Interstate 80 on Tuesday morning, and this time the National Weather Service has raised it to an advisory: visibility under a mile in Kimball County through 9 a.m. CST, with slick spots possible where the fog freezes on the road.

The [National Weather Service in Cheyenne](https://www.weather.gov/cys/) issued the Freezing Fog Advisory at 4:31 a.m. MST Tuesday, Nov. 24, covering Kimball County in Nebraska along with lower Converse, east Platte and east Laramie counties in Wyoming. The advisory runs until 8 a.m. MST, which is 9 a.m. CST. An advisory means the hazard is expected and likely — a step up from [Monday morning's Special Weather Statement](/news/freezing-fog-cuts-visibility-near-kimball-monday-morning) for the same Nebraska county.

## What we know

- **Where:** Kimball County, on I-80 from roughly mile marker 20 to 24 near the Wyoming line — the [Kimball](/cities/kimball) interchanges at Exit 20 (Neb. 71, Kimball West, mile 20.7) and Exit 22 (Kimball East, mile 22.7). Sidney and the rest of Cheyenne County are not listed in this advisory.
- **When:** In effect Tuesday morning, Nov. 24, from the 4:31 a.m. MST issuance until 8 a.m. MST (9 a.m. CST).
- **What:** Freezing fog with visibility below one mile, per NWS Cheyenne. The Weather Service warns that poor visibility could make driving hazardous and that the fog can freeze on pavement, bridges and overpasses.
- **Who confirmed it:** The [National Weather Service in Cheyenne](https://www.weather.gov/cys/), via its active-alerts feed checked Tuesday morning.
- **Status:** Active now. As of about 5:30 a.m. CST Tuesday, the advisory remains in effect and no crashes or closures are listed for I-80 in Kimball County on [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/).

## Why it matters

Kimball County holds the westernmost Nebraska miles of I-80, the gateway for Thanksgiving-week drivers crossing the state line — and freezing fog is the quietest hazard on the corridor. Unlike snow, it can glaze a bridge deck with no precipitation and no warning, and visibility can drop from clear to near-zero within a single mile. NWS instruction for this advisory is simple: slow down, use headlights and leave plenty of distance ahead. High-profile vehicles, campers and holiday trailers running the high, windy [Sidney-to-Kimball stretch](/news/sidney-kimball-last-60-miles) feel sudden visibility drops the hardest. Our [Thanksgiving-week corridor outlook](/news/thanksgiving-week-on-i-80-dry-stretch-cold-nights-four-work-zones-to-watch) called for mostly dry roads statewide; this fog is again the exception at the west end.

## What happens next

Recheck the [NWS Cheyenne office](https://www.weather.gov/cys/) and the live [Nebraska 511 map](https://511.nebraska.gov/) before rolling west of Sidney — fog often lifts within an hour of sunrise, but it can also thicken or refreeze on shaded overpasses. If the advisory extends or a crash closes lanes, we will update this story.
