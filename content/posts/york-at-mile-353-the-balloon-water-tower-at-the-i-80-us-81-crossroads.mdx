York is the kind of interstate town that rewards a deliberate stop: the exit is close to the city, the services are clustered, and the landmark is visible from a distance. It is also the place where I-80 meets US-81, the north-south route that ties central Nebraska to the interstate.

## The exits

**Exit 353 — York and US-81.** The main entrance to the city and the most useful exit for fuel, food, lodging and downtown. US-81 runs north-south through York and meets I-80 here, with Geneva to the south. Henderson and Bradshaw are served by exits 342 and 348, not by US-81 north of York.

**Exit 342 — Henderson and Bradshaw.** The eastbound-side interchange for Henderson and Bradshaw, west and southwest of York.

**Exit 348 — Bradshaw.** The westbound-side interchange for Bradshaw and the rural York County roads.

**Exit 360 — Waco.** The eastern approach, serving Waco and the rural roads on York's east side.

## The city

York is the York County seat. The 2020 census counted 8,066 people, making it one of the larger towns on the Nebraska stretch between Lincoln and Grand Island. The city's most recognizable feature is the water tower painted as a hot-air balloon. The "YORK" lettering makes it a landmark that is hard to miss from the north side of town.

York University, a private Christian university founded in 1890, is part of the city's identity and brings a steady flow of students, families and events through the exits.

Downtown is north of the interstate, around the courthouse square and the older commercial blocks. It is a real downtown, not just an exit ramp: the city offices, the library, local restaurants and the county courthouse are all part of the same compact grid.

## Why York matters on the corridor

- **The US-81 crossroads.** North-south traffic from central Nebraska and Kansas meets I-80 here. That makes York a natural stop for travelers who are switching routes, not just passing through.
- **Between the hubs.** Lincoln is roughly 50 miles east and Grand Island roughly 45–50 miles west. York's service cluster is a practical midpoint for fuel and food on that stretch.
- **A real town stop.** Unlike the bare interchange exits farther west, York's main exit leads directly into a county seat with a hospital, schools, a university and a downtown. If you need more than a gas station, this is the exit.
- **Weather split.** The NWS Hastings office covers this part of the corridor. In fall and winter, the difference between a clear drive in Lincoln and fog or ice around York can be significant; check the forecast before committing to the next stretch.

## Before you drive

1. [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) for construction, lane closures and incidents around exits 342–360.
2. The [NWS Hastings](https://www.weather.gov/gid/) forecast, especially for fog and the first ice of the season.
3. Fuel before you leave town if you are heading west toward Grand Island or east toward Lincoln; the smaller exits between here and those cities are not full-service.
4. The [City of York](https://cityofyork.ne.gov/) calendar if you are stopping for a city meeting or event.
