Seward is a county seat with a strong civic habit: the town has celebrated the Fourth of July almost every year since 1868. The interstate runs south of the courthouse square, close enough that the exit is a real entrance to the city rather than a distant spur.

## The exits

**Exit 379 — Seward and Nebraska 15.** The main entrance. Nebraska 15 runs north into Seward and south toward the county's farm country; this is the exit for downtown, Concordia and the hospital.

**Exit 373 — Goehner.** The western approach, serving Goehner and the rural roads north of the interstate.

**Exit 382 — Milford and US-6.** The eastern approach, useful for Milford, the Seward County industrial edge and US-6 traffic.

**Exit 388 — Pleasant Dale and Nebraska 103.** The far eastern exit, which also serves the Crete area to the south.

## The city

Seward is the Seward County seat and part of the Lincoln metropolitan area. The 2020 census counted 7,643 people. The courthouse square is the center of downtown: the county courthouse, the older commercial blocks and the city's civic buildings are all within a few blocks.

The city's identity is tied to the Fourth of July. Seward has celebrated Independence Day since 1868, and the city calls itself Nebraska's 4th of July City. The annual celebration is a major regional event, with a parade, food, music and a full day of programming around the holiday. If you are visiting for it, plan lodging and parking well ahead; the town fills up.

Concordia University, Nebraska, is on the north side of Seward. The Lutheran university is a year-round presence and one of the city's largest institutions. St. Gregory the Great Seminary is also in the area.

## Why Seward matters on the corridor

- **The Lincoln edge.** Seward is close enough to Lincoln to function as a commuter and university town, but far enough out to keep its own county-seat identity. The interstate makes both possible.
- **A downtown exit.** Exit 379 leads to a real courthouse square with local businesses, not just a truck-stop cluster. It is a useful stop if you want a walk and a meal rather than a drive-through.
- **The Fourth of July.** The city's signature event is not a generic summer festival; it is a tradition that dates to the 19th century. That history is part of why Seward has a stronger identity than its population alone would suggest.
- **Weather and travel.** The NWS Omaha/Valley office covers this stretch. In winter, the Seward exits are close enough to Lincoln that conditions can change quickly between the city and the open highway.

## Before you drive

1. [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) for lane closures and incidents around exits 373–388.
2. The [NWS Omaha/Valley](https://www.weather.gov/oax/) forecast, especially for snow, ice and wind.
3. Fuel in Seward or Lincoln; the smaller exits east and west are not full-service.
4. The [City of Seward](https://cityofsewardne.gov/) calendar and the [Fourth of July](https://www.julyfourthseward.com/) schedule if you are visiting.
