Nebraska is a driving state, and I-80 is the reason. But there is another way across: Amtrak's California Zephyr, the daily long-distance train between Chicago and the San Francisco Bay Area, cuts through the state on a route that predates the interstate.

It is not a commuter service, and it does not parallel I-80 for most of Nebraska. It is, however, a useful option for students, people who cannot drive, and anyone who wants to see the Platte valley without watching the bumper in front of them.

## Where the Zephyr stops in Nebraska

The train enters Nebraska from Iowa at Omaha and makes five stops before crossing into Colorado:

| Station | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Omaha | Missouri River crossing; connects to the Omaha metro |
| Lincoln | State capital; the busiest Nebraska stop |
| Hastings | About 25 miles south of I-80 |
| Holdrege | South-central Nebraska |
| McCook | Southwest Nebraska, near the Kansas border |

West of Lincoln, the route follows the Platte River valley through Hastings, Holdrege and McCook. That is south of the interstate, which means the train serves a different set of towns than I-80 does. If your destination is Kearney, North Platte or Ogallala, the train will not get you there.

## The schedule reality

The Zephyr runs daily in both directions, but the times are not designed around Nebraska. Some stops are in the middle of the night. Delays of several hours are common on a 2,400-mile route, especially in winter when the train shares track with freight.

Check [Amtrak's official schedule](https://www.amtrak.com/california-zephyr-train) before you plan anything. For a rough real-time picture, the unofficial [Amtraker](https://amtraker.com/) site tracks train 5 (westbound) and train 6 (eastbound). We use it for context, not as an official source.

## How it compares to driving

**Driving I-80** from Omaha to the Wyoming line is roughly 454 miles and six to seven hours in good conditions. You control the stops, and you can reach Kearney, North Platte, Ogallala, Sidney and Kimball directly.

**The Zephyr** takes longer and serves a different corridor. Omaha to McCook is an overnight-ish ride depending on the direction. You will need a plan for the last mile at your destination; most Nebraska stations are small and do not have rental cars waiting.

**Cost** varies wildly. A coach ticket booked early can be cheaper than fuel and a motel; a last-minute sleeper can cost more than a flight. Compare before you decide.

## When the train is the better call

- You are traveling to Denver or further west and do not want to drive the mountain passes.
- You are a student going between Lincoln and Hastings or Holdrege.
- Winter weather has closed I-80 and you can wait for a train instead of a plow.
- You want a laptop-friendly, hands-free trip.

## When it is not

- Your destination is Kearney, North Platte, Ogallala or any I-80 town not named Omaha or Lincoln.
- You need to arrive at a specific time. The Zephyr is not a reliable same-day business option.
- You are traveling with a car. Amtrak's Nebraska stops do not offer drive-on service.

## The bottom line

The Zephyr is a real alternative for a narrow set of trips, and a pleasant one if your schedule is flexible. It is not an I-80 replacement. Nebraska's interstate exists because the state is wide and the train does not go where most of the corridor goes.
