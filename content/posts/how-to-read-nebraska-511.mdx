Nebraska 511 is the first thing to open before any winter drive on Interstate 80, and the first thing most people close in frustration. The official map is dense, the icons are small, and the layers are not labeled for someone trying to answer one question: *is my stretch of I-80 open, and what is it going to be like?*

Here is how the map actually works, which layers are worth your time, and where 511 stops and other sources have to take over.

## What 511 is

Nebraska 511 is run by the Nebraska Department of Transportation through the Castle Rock ITS "CARS" traveler-information platform. It pulls together incidents, construction, road weather, cameras, plows, message signs and rest-area status into one map and mobile app.

It is free and it is official. It is also **view-only**. NDOT does not publish a supported public API for incident or camera data, and the bulk XML feeds require a data agreement with Castle Rock. That matters if you are building a product on top of it, but for a driver it just means: use the map, the app, or dial 511.

## The layers that matter, in order

**1. Incidents and closures.** This is the core. Crashes, disabled vehicles, debris, lane closures and full closures appear here. Zoom in on your stretch rather than trusting the statewide view — a single icon can represent several miles of affected roadway.

**2. Winter road conditions.** NDOT rates segments as dry, wet, partially covered, covered or closed. The ratings are updated by maintenance districts, not continuously. A segment marked "partially covered" at 6 a.m. may be bare by 10 a.m., and vice versa when the wind picks up.

**3. Cameras.** Camera icons show still images at many interchanges and bridges. They are useful for one thing: confirming what the pavement actually looks like right now. Coverage is not universal, and images can lag. Do not treat a camera as a live guarantee for the next 20 miles.

**4. Plows.** During storms, the plow layer shows where NDOT trucks are working. It is a snapshot, not a promise. A plow that passed your exit 40 minutes ago is not evidence that the ramp is clear.

**5. Rest areas and welcome centers.** Useful for planning stops, especially on the long western stretch where services are spread out. Status can change during storms.

**6. Message signs and travel times.** Helpful for verifying what the overhead signs are saying before you reach them.

## What 511 will not tell you

- **It is not a live crash feed.** Incidents are entered by dispatchers and maintenance staff. A crash that happened five minutes ago may not be on the map yet.
- **It does not replace a weather forecast.** Road conditions tell you what has happened. The National Weather Service tells you what is coming. On the western corridor, wind and blowing snow can close a road that looks fine on the map.
- **It does not give you a detour.** When I-80 closes, NDOT may publish detour guidance, but local roads are not designed for interstate volumes. Do not assume a parallel county road is a safe shortcut.
- **It is not a news source.** The State Patrol's newsroom is where crashes, pursuits and fatalities are confirmed. Use 511 for conditions, NSP for confirmation, and your own reporting for context.

## The practical pre-drive routine

1. Open [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) and zoom to your exact stretch — Omaha to Lincoln, Lincoln to Grand Island, Grand Island to Kearney, Kearney to North Platte, North Platte to Ogallala, Ogallala to Sidney, Sidney to Kimball.
2. Turn on incidents and winter conditions. Check cameras at the interchanges you will actually pass.
3. Pull up the [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/) forecast for the county you are driving into, not just the one you are leaving.
4. Check the [NSP newsroom](https://statepatrol.nebraska.gov/divisions/office-superintendent/news) and [NDOT news](https://dot.nebraska.gov/news-events/news-releases/) for anything structural — a bridge project, a long-term closure, a known incident.
5. If the road is closed, stop at the nearest town and wait. Do not drive around barricades. Nebraska's closures exist because the alternative is a multi-hour rescue in a whiteout.

## If you do not have a smartphone

Dial **511** from anywhere in Nebraska for the automated traveler-information line. The State Patrol's emergency number is **\*55** from a cell phone. Both are free and work when the map will not load.

The 511 app is available for [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nebraska-511/id921773122) and Android under the package `crc.carsapp.ne`.

## The bottom line

Nebraska 511 is the best official snapshot of the corridor. It is not a live sensor, a forecast, or a news desk. The drivers who get into trouble are usually the ones who checked it once at the start of a 300-mile drive and assumed nothing changed.

Check it before you leave. Check it again at Grand Island. Check it again at North Platte. That is the routine.
