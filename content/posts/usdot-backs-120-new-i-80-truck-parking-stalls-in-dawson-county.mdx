Interstate 80 in Dawson County is in line for about 120 new public truck parking stalls after federal officials selected a Nebraska Department of Transportation proposal for a $7.46 million grant, according to NDOT.

The award was announced by NDOT on Nov. 5 in Lincoln as part of a package in which the U.S. Department of Transportation selected seven Nebraska projects for up to $59.6 million in federal funding. The I-80 truck parking project, between Elm Creek and Overton, is the only highway project in the list — the other six fund rail safety and reliability work.

## What we know

- **What:** A $7.46 million Nationally Significant Multimodal Freight and Highway Projects (INFRA) Track 2 grant for commercial truck parking on I-80 in Dawson County. NDOT says the total project investment is $15.5 million in federal and state funds.
- **Where:** Along I-80 in Dawson County, between Elm Creek and Overton. NDOT did not give mile markers, exit numbers or a side of the highway in the Nov. 5 release.
- **When:** Announced Nov. 5, 2026. NDOT did not announce a construction start or completion date.
- **Who confirmed it:** [Nebraska Department of Transportation](https://dot.nebraska.gov/media/tgzmld3a/20261105-comm-usdot-grants.pdf), citing a USDOT selection. NDOT Director Vicki Kramer is quoted in the release.
- **Status:** Selected for funding, not under construction. The release describes the stalls as planned capacity.

One detail needs a careful read: the release says the project "will add approximately 120 new truck parking stalls" in one paragraph, while Kramer's quoted statement in the same release says "approximately 92 stalls of parking capacity." The desk is reporting both figures as written and has not smoothed them into one number. The total project cost is stated consistently as $15.5 million.

## Why it matters

Anyone who drives I-80 after dark in central Nebraska knows the pattern: ramps, rest areas and private lots fill early, and trucks end up parked on shoulders and interchange ramps. INFRA Track 2 money specifically targets that national shortage of commercial parking by paying for safe, accessible public stalls, according to NDOT.

Dawson County sits in the long freight run between [Lexington](/cities/lexington) and [Kearney](/cities/kearney), where overnight truck traffic is heavy and alternative parking is spaced miles apart. Our guide to [the 100-mile gaps in fuel, food and shelter](/news/i-80-service-gaps-nebraska) describes how thin services get on exactly this kind of stretch — a gap that matters more for a driver under hours-of-service rules than for a car that can push on to the next town.

NDOT's framing matches that: "I-80 is a critical freight corridor for Nebraska and the nation, and safe truck parking is an important part of keeping freight moving," Kramer said in the release.

## What happens next

This is a funding selection, not a work-zone notice. There are no lane closures, detours or opening dates attached yet. When NDOT advertises the project, assigns a contractor or posts it to [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/), that will be the signal that construction timing is real — check our explainer on [where NDOT posts I-80 construction](/news/ndot-i-80-construction-nebraska) for where to look.

The same federal package includes up to $7 million for California Zephyr corridor reliability work — switch heaters, hot-air blowers and turnout replacements on BNSF infrastructure across Nebraska, Iowa and Colorado aimed at reducing frozen-switch incidents in winter, according to the release. That matters for the passenger-rail side of the corridor we covered in [Amtrak's California Zephyr across Nebraska](/news/amtrak-california-zephyr-nebraska). Other selections include grade-crossing studies in Crete and western Nebraska and track rehabilitation in southwest Nebraska and between Lincoln and Nebraska City.

For truckers planning a run on Nov. 6, nothing changes: use existing public and private parking, check [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) before a night run, and expect the Dawson County stalls to go through design and bidding before cones appear. We will update this story when NDOT posts a timeline.
