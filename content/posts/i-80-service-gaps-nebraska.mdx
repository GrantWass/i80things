Driving I-80 from Omaha to Lincoln, you can stop every few minutes. Driving from North Platte to Ogallala, you can drive for the better part of an hour without seeing a town. The difference is not just scenery. It is fuel, food, cell coverage and shelter.

This is a practical guide to the service islands on the Nebraska corridor — the places where you should stop even if you think you can make it.

## The short gaps (Omaha to Kearney)

- **Omaha to Lincoln:** about 50 miles. Dense services at Gretna, Ashland, Waverly and the Lincoln exits.
- **Lincoln to York:** about 46 miles. Seward and Milford have fuel; Beaver Crossing is smaller.
- **York to Grand Island:** about 40 miles. Aurora is a full-service stop at Exit 332.
- **Grand Island to Kearney:** about 40 miles. Wood River and Gibbon are smaller but usable.

If you are driving this stretch, you do not need a plan. If you are towing or low on fuel, the York and Aurora exits are the easy ones.

## The long gaps (Kearney to Kimball)

**Kearney to North Platte — about 95 miles.** Lexington (Exits 237/248) and Gothenburg (Exit 211) break it up. Lexington has the most services; Gothenburg has fuel and food. Cozad (Exit 222) is another option. Do not assume every exit has a open gas station after 10 p.m.

**North Platte to Ogallala — about 51 miles.** Paxton (Exit 145) is the intermediate stop, with limited services. North Platte is the last full-service city before Ogallala; fill up there if you are heading west.

**Ogallala to Sidney — about 67 miles.** Chappell (Exit 85) is the only real intermediate stop, and it is small. Ogallala has fuel, food and motels. Sidney has the next full cluster.

**Sidney to Kimball — about 37 miles.** Kimball is the last Nebraska stop before Wyoming. Services are limited; do not count on finding an open mechanic.

## Hospitals and emergency care

The corridor's full-service hospitals are concentrated in the metros:

- **Omaha:** multiple Level I and Level II trauma centers
- **Lincoln:** Bryan Health and CHI Health St. Elizabeth
- **Grand Island:** CHI Health St. Francis
- **Kearney:** CHI Health Good Samaritan
- **North Platte:** Great Plains Health
- **Sidney:** Sidney Regional Medical Center
- **Ogallala and Kimball:** critical-access hospitals

If you are in a crash west of North Platte, you may be transported to North Platte or Sidney depending on severity and weather. That is another reason not to push through a storm.

## Rest areas

Nebraska operates rest areas on I-80, and 511 shows their status. They are not a substitute for a town: some have limited hours, and during winter storms they can fill up or close. Use them for a stretch, not for an overnight in a blizzard.

## The winter rule

In good weather, the gaps are an inconvenience. In winter, they are the whole risk. A car that runs out of fuel at mile 145 in a whiteout becomes a rescue operation. A driver who passes the last open motel in Ogallala because "Sidney is only an hour" may spend the night in a ditch.

The rule is simple: **west of Kearney, fill up at every town with a station, and stop before dark if the weather is turning.** The interstate will still be there tomorrow.
