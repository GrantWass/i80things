North Platte is the capital of the corridor's empty middle: a city of about 23,000 that anchors a stretch of I-80 with very little else around it. It is also a railroad town, and the railroad is impossible to miss.

Bailey Yard, Union Pacific's classification yard, stretches for miles along the north side of town. The Golden Spike Tower gives visitors a view over the hump and the endless lines of freight cars. For travelers, the yard is a reminder of why the corridor exists: freight moves through Nebraska at all hours, and the interstate is only part of the picture.

## The exits

**Mile 177 — US-83 / south North Platte.** The main commercial exit, with the fuel and food clusters most travelers use.

**Mile 179 — central North Platte.** Local access to the city's core and the hospital.

**Mile 183 — north side.** Convenient for Bailey Yard, the Golden Spike Tower and the industrial area.

**Mile 186 and 190 — east North Platte.** The city's eastern edge and the approach to the Platte valley.

## The last decision point

West of North Platte, the corridor changes. Ogallala is about 51 miles away, and the intermediate stops are small. If you are low on fuel, low on time or worried about weather, North Platte is the place to stop.

Great Plains Health is the regional hospital. The city has the last large cluster of motels, repair shops and 24-hour services before Ogallala and Sidney.

## Weather on the western corridor

The National Weather Service office in [North Platte](https://www.weather.gov/lbf/) covers this stretch. Winter storms here are often wind-driven: the snow may be light, but the visibility can drop to nothing across the open ground between North Platte and Ogallala.

When the forecast calls for wind and snow, the closure risk is highest west of town. Check [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) for the segment and be prepared to stop in North Platte rather than push on.

## What to see if you stop

- **Golden Spike Tower and Bailey Yard.** The best view of the railroad that built the corridor.
- **Buffalo Bill Ranch State Historical Park.** The scout's home and the setting for his famous Wild West show.
- **The Platte River.** The shallow, braided river that guided the Oregon Trail, the railroad and the interstate.

North Platte is not a pass-through if you have time. It is the corridor's best explanation of itself.
