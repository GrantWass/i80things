Lincoln is the largest city on the corridor that I-80 does not actually enter. The interstate runs along the city's north edge, close enough to serve commuters and university traffic but far enough that most travelers see only the exits.

That makes Lincoln a junction town. Four interchanges connect the interstate to the capital, the university and the roads south and east.

## The exits

**Mile 395 — Waverly.** The eastern edge of the Lincoln metro, serving Waverly and the industrial land along the interstate. A common commuter entry point from the east.

**Mile 397 — northeast Lincoln.** The local-access interchange for the city's northeast side, including the areas around the airport and the Interstate Industrial Park.

**Mile 399 — I-180 / downtown.** The most important exit for visitors. I-180 runs south from the interstate into downtown Lincoln, ending near the state capitol and the Haymarket. This is the exit for University of Nebraska–Lincoln events, the Lied Center and the state government complex.

**Mile 401 — US-77 / Homestead Expressway.** The route south toward Beatrice and southeast Nebraska, and the western edge of the Lincoln metro. If you are bypassing the city, this is where the interstate bends northwest toward Seward and Grand Island.

## The commuter pattern

Lincoln's traffic runs opposite the usual metro pattern on I-80: the heaviest volumes are often commuters entering the city from the east and west in the morning and leaving in the afternoon. The I-180 interchange is the pressure point during Husker home games and legislative sessions.

If you are passing through, stay on I-80. There is no reason to leave the interstate unless you are stopping. If you are heading downtown, take Exit 399 and follow I-180 south.

## Services and alternatives

Lincoln has the corridor's second-largest cluster of hospitals, fuel and hotels. Bryan Health and CHI Health St. Elizabeth are the main hospital systems. The city also has a dense local road grid, so a closure on I-80 does not trap you the way a closure west of Kearney can.

The trade-off is traffic. During a Husker game, a winter storm or a major downtown event, the I-180 spur and the north Lincoln interchanges can back up for miles.

## Winter on the north edge

The interstate north of Lincoln is exposed farmland. Wind-driven snow can close lanes between Waverly and the US-77 interchange even when the city streets are clear. Check [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) for the segment and the [NWS Omaha/Valley](https://www.weather.gov/oax/) forecast before you commit.
