A hit-and-run on Interstate 80 near Lincoln turned into a two-part pursuit at speeds topping 110 mph on Thursday, ending with an arrest in northeast Lincoln after the Nebraska State Patrol's aircraft tracked the fleeing car from above.

According to the [Nebraska State Patrol](https://statepatrol.nebraska.gov/pursuit-leads-arrest-northeast-lincoln), the sequence began just after 12:00 p.m. on Nov. 5, when troopers received a report of a Chevrolet Impala that had been speeding on I-80, struck the back of a trailer and kept driving eastbound. About 15 minutes later, a trooper spotted the car traveling eastbound on I-80 in Lincoln and tried to stop it. The driver accelerated instead, and the pursuit was on.

## What we know

- **Where:** Eastbound I-80 in Lincoln, then southbound 56th Street, then eastbound Highway 6 toward Waverly, ending near 71st and Douglas in Lincoln. The patrol did not release a mile marker for the initial hit-and-run.
- **When:** Just after 12:00 p.m. on Thursday, Nov. 5. The pursuit itself lasted about 12 minutes, according to NSP.
- **Status:** One man is in custody. No injuries were reported in the patrol's release.
- **Agency:** Nebraska State Patrol, which published the account on Nov. 5.

Speeds exceeded 110 mph on I-80 before the Impala exited onto southbound 56th Street, the patrol said. As the car approached high-traffic areas on 56th, the trooper broke off the ground pursuit out of concern for public safety — a decision the patrol described explicitly in its release.

The car resurfaced a short time later, speeding eastbound on Highway 6 out of Lincoln. A second stop attempt failed the same way, but this time the NSP AirWing was overhead and took over the pursuit from the air, following the Impala toward Waverly as it turned around and headed back into Lincoln. During that stretch, the driver is suspected of throwing a gun out of the car onto Highway 6 near 98th Street. Troopers recovered the firearm, according to the release.

Pilots tracked the car to the area of 71st and Douglas, where it parked and a single occupant got out and walked away. The air crew directed troopers on the ground to the spot, and the driver was taken into custody without further incident.

## Why it matters

The noon hour on I-80 through [Lincoln](/cities/lincoln) is one of the corridor's busiest windows, with commuter and freight traffic mixing between exits 395 and 401. A 110-mph pursuit in that flow is exactly the kind of event that closes lanes without warning — and the patrol's call to discontinue the ground chase on 56th Street shows how troopers weigh catching a suspect against endangering everyone else on the road.

The case also illustrates why the AirWing matters on the eastern corridor: once the helicopter or plane has the car, ground units can back off, follow at a distance and move in only when the car stops — which is what happened here.

## What happens next

The driver, identified by NSP as James Bernard, 44, of Thornton, Colorado, was arrested for flight to avoid arrest, willful reckless driving, possession of a deadly weapon by a prohibited person, possession of a deadly weapon while committing a felony, possession of a controlled substance and other charges. Court proceedings in Lancaster County will determine what comes next; the patrol's release did not list a court date.
