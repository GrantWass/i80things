Eighteen people were killed in 17 traffic crashes on Nebraska roads in October, according to the Nebraska Department of Transportation, which released its monthly fatality toll on Nov. 16. The figures cover all public roads in the state, including Interstate 80, and come from NDOT's Traffic Engineering Division with data available through Nov. 15.

## What we know

- **What:** 18 traffic deaths in 17 fatal crashes statewide during October 2026, per NDOT.
- **Where:** One death on the interstate system, 10 on other highways and seven on local roads. NDOT's release does not name the highway in the interstate case, so it cannot be tied to a specific mile of I-80. Thirteen of the 18 deaths were in rural locations, using the Federal Highway Administration definition of rural as outside areas of 5,000 or more people.
- **When:** October 2026; the toll was published Nov. 16, 2026.
- **Who confirmed it:** NDOT's Traffic Engineering Division, in its [October Traffic Fatality Toll release](https://dot.nebraska.gov/media/ldtmbqmm/20261116-traffic-oct-fatality.pdf).
- **Current status:** The October count is based on information available through Nov. 15 and feeds NDOT's running daily count at [ndot.info/tollcompare](https://ndot.info/tollcompare).

The victims included 14 vehicle occupants, three pedestrians, one motorcyclist and one person on an all-terrain vehicle, NDOT said. NDOT's victim categories overlap — they describe the kinds of victims rather than adding to the 18 total. Of the 14 vehicle occupants killed, seven were not using seat belts, four were belted and three were marked unknown. [10/11 News reported](https://www.1011now.com/2026/11/17/18-killed-nebraska-traffic-crashes-october-ndot-says/) that half of the vehicle occupants who died were unbelted, matching NDOT's breakdown.

## Why it matters

The seat-belt split is the detail that matters most for I-80 drivers. For the year to date, only 53 of the 138 vehicle occupants killed in Nebraska in 2026 were wearing seat belts, according to NDOT. On a high-speed, long-distance corridor like I-80 — where Omaha-to-Kimball trips run through long rural stretches with limited places to stop — an unbelted occupant faces far worse odds in a rollover or multi-vehicle crash. Thirteen of October's 18 deaths were rural, the same pattern that dominates the interstate west of Lincoln.

The broader trend is modestly better than recent years. NDOT's comparison table puts January–October 2026 at 174 deaths in 158 fatal crashes, compared with 184 deaths in the same period of 2025 — about a 6 percent decline. The five-year context is wider: 222 deaths in the same span of 2024 and a 2022–2025 average of 199. October itself was nearly flat year over year, with 17 deaths in October 2025 versus 18 this October.

## What happens next

NDOT updates its running tally daily at [ndot.info/tollcompare](https://ndot.info/tollcompare), and the November toll typically arrives in mid-December. If you drive I-80 regularly, two habits cover most of what this report is really saying: buckle every seat on every trip, and treat rural highway segments — night driving, two-lane crossovers in work zones, county-road intersections near interchanges — with extra margin. For current conditions before any trip, check [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/).
