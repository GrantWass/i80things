The last 60 miles of Nebraska's I-80 run through the high plains of Cheyenne and Kimball counties, where the towns are small, the wind is constant and the Wyoming line is always in the back of your mind.

Sidney is the biggest service cluster on this stretch. Kimball is the last Nebraska town of any size before Wyoming. The space between them is where the corridor feels most like the West.

## Sidney (mile 55–59)

Sidney is the Cheyenne County seat and the home of Cabela's, the outdoor retailer that started here and became a destination. The city has fuel, food, motels and a hospital.

- **Exit 55** — the western approach to Sidney.
- **Exit 59** — the main commercial exit and the easiest access to Cabela's and the hotel cluster.

Sidney is where you should fill up if you are heading west. The next full cluster is Kimball, and the services there are limited.

## Kimball (mile 20–29)

Kimball is the last Nebraska town of any size before Wyoming: fuel, a few restaurants, a critical-access hospital and a whole lot of sky.

- **Exit 20** — the western approach to Kimball.
- **Exit 22** — the main town exit.
- **Exit 29** — Dix and the eastern county roads.

From Kimball, I-80 climbs toward the Wyoming line and the high country beyond. In winter, this is where wind-driven snow can close the road with little warning.

## Why this stretch is different

The land is high, open and exposed. There are fewer trees, fewer towns and fewer places to shelter. A crash or a whiteout here can close the interstate for hours, and the detour may be a two-lane road with no services.

The National Weather Service office in [Cheyenne](https://www.weather.gov/cys/) covers the western edge. When a high wind or winter storm warning is issued, expect the western stretch to be exposed; check 511 before committing to it.

## Before you drive it

1. Check [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) for the segment and the western cameras.
2. Check the [NWS Cheyenne](https://www.weather.gov/cys/) forecast and any wind advisory.
3. Fill up in Sidney or Kimball. Do not assume an open station at the smaller exits.
4. Carry a winter kit, even in shoulder season. The weather here can change faster than the forecast.

If the road closes, stop in Sidney or Kimball. Do not take a county road around a barricade. The local grid is not designed for interstate traffic, and the wind can erase the road in minutes.
