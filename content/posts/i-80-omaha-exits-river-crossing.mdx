Omaha is where I-80 begins in Nebraska — or ends, depending on which way you are driving. The interstate crosses the Missouri River from Council Bluffs at mile 454, runs through the city's south and west sides, and then turns toward Lincoln and the Platte valley.

For a corridor that is mostly rural, Omaha is a different animal: four to six lanes, heavy commuter traffic, two loop interchanges, a downtown spur and a river bridge that can back up for miles. If you are passing through, the exits matter.

## The main interchanges

**Mile 454 — Missouri River.** The bridge into Council Bluffs, Iowa, connecting to I-29 and the Omaha metro's eastern edge. This is the corridor's eastern terminus and one of its most weather-sensitive crossings: ice, wind and crashes on the bridge ripple back through both states.

**Mile 452 — I-480 / downtown.** The I-480 spur is the direct route into downtown Omaha, the Old Market, the CHI Health Center and the riverfront. It is short, busy and easy to miss if you are not in the right lane.

**Mile 449 — central Omaha.** A key exit for the city's midtown and the roads that feed the University of Nebraska Medical Center and the Dundee-Benson area. Local traffic uses this interchange heavily.

**Mile 445 — I-680.** The north loop. I-680 connects west Omaha to the airport and the northern suburbs, and it is the recommended route to Eppley Airfield from the west.

**Mile 439 — Gretna / west Omaha.** The metro's growth edge, with the outlet mall, new subdivisions and a heavy commuter merge. This is where the interstate starts to feel rural again.

## Where it backs up

- **The Missouri River bridge** during rush hour, bad weather or an Iowa-side incident.
- **The I-80/I-680 interchange** at mile 445, especially westbound in the afternoon.
- **West Omaha around mile 439** as commuters merge from Gretna and the industrial parks.
- **I-480** whenever there is a downtown event, a Creighton or UNL game, or a riverfront festival.

## Passing through vs. stopping

If you are driving the corridor and do not need Omaha, I-80 itself stays south of downtown. You can bypass the city core entirely. If you are heading to the airport, take I-680 north. If you are heading downtown, take I-480.

If you are stopping, the metro has the corridor's densest cluster of fuel, food, hospitals and hotels. It also has the most alternatives when I-80 is jammed: I-680, I-480, US-75 and the city's arterial grid.

## Weather and the river

The Omaha area gets the eastern corridor's worst ice events when a storm tracks along the Platte and warm air aloft produces freezing rain. The river bridge and the I-680 interchange are usually the first places to slow.

Check [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) before you cross and the [NWS Omaha/Valley](https://www.weather.gov/oax/) forecast for timing. For the rest of the corridor, see the [mile marker guide](/news/i-80-mile-markers-nebraska-explained).
