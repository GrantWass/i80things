Kimball is the last Nebraska city of any size before the Wyoming line, and it looks the part: a compact county seat on the high plains, with the interstate running just south of town and the old Lincoln Highway running through it. The wind is part of the welcome.

## The exits

**Exit 20 — Kimball west and Nebraska 71.** The western approach, with Nebraska 71 running north toward the Panhandle and south toward the Colorado line.

**Exit 22 — Kimball east.** The main entrance to the city from I-80, connecting to US-30 and the downtown grid.

**Exit 29 — Dix.** The eastern approach, serving Dix and the rural roads of eastern Kimball County.

## The city

Kimball is the Kimball County seat. The 2020 census counted 2,290 people, and the city sits at roughly 4,725 feet above sea level. It calls itself the "High Point of Nebraska," a reference to its elevation and to nearby Panorama Point, the state's highest point at 5,424 feet. Panorama Point is south of Kimball, not inside the city; the distinction matters if you are chasing the state high point.

The downtown is compact and walkable, with the county courthouse, city offices, local businesses and the kind of older commercial blocks that grew along the railroad and the Lincoln Highway. US-30 still runs through town, parallel to I-80, and is the better road for seeing the city rather than bypassing it.

The city operates Four Winds Golf Course, City Park and Gotte Park, and the surrounding county is a mix of agriculture, wind energy and small oil and gas producers. Wind turbines are part of the western Nebraska landscape here, alongside the grain elevators and cattle country.

## Why Kimball matters on the corridor

- **The Wyoming approach.** Westbound drivers are close to the state line here, and the weather can change fast. Kimball is the last full-service stop before the climb into Wyoming, so it is a sensible place to fuel up and check the forecast.
- **A county seat, not just an exit.** The city has a courthouse, schools, a newspaper and a downtown. It is a real community, not a travel-center invention.
- **The high point.** Panorama Point is the reason many travelers detour south of town. The marker is simple, the view is wide, and the wind is usually doing something.
- **High-plains weather.** The NWS Cheyenne office covers this corner of Nebraska. Snow, blowing snow and sudden visibility drops are the winter hazards; in summer, the same open country means heat, wind and dust.

## Before you drive

1. [Nebraska 511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) for closures and incidents around exits 20–29 and the Wyoming line.
2. The [NWS Cheyenne](https://www.weather.gov/cys/) forecast, especially for wind and winter weather.
3. Fuel and food in Kimball before heading west; do not assume the smaller exits toward the state line are full-service.
4. The [City of Kimball](https://kimballne.org/) site for city meetings and community events.
