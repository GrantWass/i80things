Overnight drivers on I-80 between Lincoln and Omaha should plan for narrowed lanes starting Sunday: bridge joint replacement at mile marker 427 begins Nov. 8, with alternating lane closures every night from 9 p.m. to 6 a.m. for about 10 days.

The schedule is a change. According to the [Nebraska Department of Transportation](https://dot.nebraska.gov/media/h3sbjda1/20261104-d1.pdf), the work was previously set for Thursday, Nov. 4, and has been rescheduled for Sunday, Nov. 8. The contractor, Garcia Chicoine Enterprises, will start in the eastbound lanes and then move to the westbound lanes, alternating closures as the project progresses.

## What we know

- **Where:** I-80 at mile marker 427, between the Lincoln and Omaha metro areas in Cass County. Nebraska 511 describes nearby bridge work as a few miles west of the Melia area.
- **When:** Nightly from 9 p.m. to 6 a.m., starting Sunday, Nov. 8, for an anticipated 10 days.
- **Status:** Scheduled and confirmed by NDOT District 1 on Nov. 4. Daytime lanes are not affected.
- **Agency:** Nebraska Department of Transportation, District 1 (contact Tyler Chicoine, 402-418-2459).

## Why it matters

Mile 427 is squarely in the commuter and freight path between Nebraska's two largest metros — including late-night truck traffic that deliberately travels overnight to avoid daytime congestion. A 9-to-6 lane closure means those drivers will funnel into a single lane with arrow boards, cones and workers feet from live traffic.

Bridge joints are the expansion gaps that let a bridge deck flex with temperature. Replacing them is routine maintenance, but it requires cutting into the driving surface, so the work zone will have uneven pavement and sudden merges even on nights when crews appear to be elsewhere on the bridge.

## What happens next

If the 10-day estimate holds, the work should wrap around Nov. 18. Weather can shift that — overnight temperatures and rain affect this kind of paving-adjacent work — so treat the end date as approximate.

NDOT's standing advice applies here: slow down in the work zone, buckle up and put the phone down.
