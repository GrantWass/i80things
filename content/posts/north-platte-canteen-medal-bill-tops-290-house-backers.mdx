The World War II canteen at North Platte is one step closer to Congress's highest civilian honor — but it has not received it yet.

A House bill awarding a Congressional Gold Medal to the volunteers and communities behind the North Platte Canteen reached 295 co-sponsors, as KNOP reported Nov. 12. That clears the 290 co-sponsors House rules effectively require before a Gold Medal bill can move — the milestone advocates have chased all year. Rep. Adrian Smith of Nebraska introduced the House bill; Sen. Deb Fischer's companion Senate version, S.645, [passed the Senate by unanimous consent on June 8](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/645/all-info) with 76 Senate co-sponsors and has been held at the House desk since June 9, according to Congress.gov.

## What we know

- **What:** H.R.1502, the North Platte Canteen Congressional Gold Medal Act, would award the medal collectively to the individuals and communities who volunteered at or donated to the Canteen — not to any single person.
- **Where things stand:** 295 House co-sponsors as of Nov. 12, per Congress.gov via KNOP; Senate version passed 76-backed in June. Still needed: a House vote and the president's signature.
- **When:** The milestone landed around Veterans Day. North Platte Mayor Brandon Kelliher highlighted the effort at the city's Veterans Day ceremony, saying the community could receive the medal if President Donald Trump signs the legislation.
- **Who confirmed it:** Congress.gov bill records, Sen. Fischer's office, and KNOP's Nov. 12 reporting from North Platte.

## Why it matters

For [North Platte](/cities/north-platte), this is the town's origin story meeting national recognition. The Canteen operated out of the Union Pacific depot from Christmas Day 1941 through April 1, 1946, greeting troop trains that stopped in town day and night. According to Fischer's office, an estimated 55,000 people from 125 communities across Nebraska, Colorado and Kansas contributed food, money or time, serving roughly 6 million service members. "The Canteen was a beacon of hope for soldiers during such a troubling and uncertain time," Fischer said after the Senate vote.

It is also a genuine I-80 story. North Platte sits at [mile 177](/news/north-platte-i-80-bailey-yard), where the interstate meets Bailey Yard — which Union Pacific describes as the world's largest rail classification yard — and the Canteen saga is the reason many travelers already stop here. A Gold Medal would cement the town's place on the national map the way the Archway did for Kearney, giving east-west drivers one more reason to pull off the interstate.

A note of precision, because local headlines around Veterans Day have run ahead of the process: Congress has not awarded the medal yet. Senate passage plus 295 House co-sponsors means the votes are very likely there, but until the House passes the bill and it is signed into law, the Canteen's honor is pending, not secured.

## What happens next

Watch the House floor calendar: with the co-sponsor threshold cleared, the bill is eligible for consideration, and Gold Medal bills with this level of backing are typically passed by wide margins. If the House passes it, the bill goes to the president's desk. We will update this story when the House acts.
