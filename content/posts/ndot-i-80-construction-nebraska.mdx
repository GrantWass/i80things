Nebraska does not publish a clean, machine-readable feed of I-80 construction. There is no RSS for NDOT news, no public closure API, and no single page that lists every lane restriction on the corridor. The information exists, but it is spread across four places with different purposes.

If you are trying to answer "is my drive going to be delayed," here is the order that works.

## 1. Nebraska 511 — for what is happening now

[511.nebraska.gov](https://511.nebraska.gov/) is the only source with live construction and incident status. Turn on the construction layer and zoom to your stretch. Lane closures, ramp closures and work zones appear as icons; tapping one gives the description and timing where available.

511 is also where you find the camera nearest a work zone. That is often more useful than the text description, especially for a lane shift that has been moved overnight.

## 2. NDOT news releases — for what changed this week

NDOT's [news releases page](https://dot.nebraska.gov/news-events/news-releases/) is where the department announces long-term closures, bridge projects, ramp reconstructions and traffic-pattern changes. There is no RSS feed. We check it manually each weekday, and so should anyone planning a story or a haul.

Release titles are usually specific: a highway number, a county and a time window. If a release mentions I-80, read the whole thing — the useful detail is often in the second paragraph, not the headline.

## 3. NDOT project pages — for the season

The [projects hub](https://dot.nebraska.gov/projects/) links to corridor studies, environmental documents and annual construction programs by district. The district PDFs are the closest thing Nebraska has to a construction calendar: they list the projects programmed for the year, not necessarily the exact lane-closure schedule.

This is the right layer for context. If a bridge is being replaced, the project page will tell you why, how long it is scheduled to take and what the detour concept is. It will not tell you whether the left lane is closed at 7 a.m. tomorrow.

## 4. The detour PDF — for the worst case

NDOT publishes [construction detour guidance](https://dot.nebraska.gov/media/4v5lbeay/construction-detours-info.pdf) for major closures. Read it before you need it. The key point is that detours are specific routes, not "take the nearest county road." Nebraska's rural grid is not built for interstate traffic volumes, and a line of semis on a gravel road is its own emergency.

## What is not public

- **Bid lettings.** NDOT's contractor letting system, Bid Express, requires a login. Award information eventually appears in public documents, but there is no open feed.
- **Real-time work-zone schedules.** Crews move. A closure listed for "overnight" may start early or end late.
- **Camera data as a feed.** Cameras are view-only on 511. There is no public image API, and NDOT/Castle Rock terms do not grant redistribution.

## How we cover construction

When NDOT announces an I-80 project that will affect traffic, we write it up with the [mile markers](/news/i-80-mile-markers-nebraska-explained), the expected timeline and the official detour. We link the release and the project page so you can check for changes yourself.

We do not publish a construction schedule as if it were fixed. If a project is delayed — and they often are — the story gets updated.

For live conditions, start at [511](https://511.nebraska.gov/) or our [I-80 conditions page](/conditions). For the statewide picture, use our [road conditions topic page](/topics/road-conditions). For the towns and exits affected, see the [mile marker guide](/news/i-80-mile-markers-nebraska-explained).
