The band crest, done properly

Nebraska centred, one cob on each side, everything sitting in the strip above INTERSTATE — with the 80 untouched. Two versions: a filled state and an outlined one. Same cob drawing as before, scaled to 6 × 12 units, which is why it reads as texture at 64 and as corn at 260.

Version 1

Filled state

Solid paper silhouette between the cobs. The panhandle notch is the only shape information, so it carries at 64 px. This is the version to pitch.

At 64The state is 24 px wide and the cobs are 6 px slivers. The silhouette still reads.
At 260Kernel rows, husks and the panhandle notch all show. This is the money size.
KeepsINTERSTATE and 80 exactly where they are. Nothing else on the badge moves.

Version 2

Outlined state

Same layout, but the state is a stroke instead of a fill. Lighter, more like a map inset, and it stops the badge looking like a solid block of paper. The tradeoff: the outline starts to break up at 64 px.

At 64The 1.4 stroke becomes hairline and disappears against the ring.
At 260Clean and map-like; pairs well with the thin type above it.
KeepsThe band breathing room, at the cost of small-size reliability.

Detail

What the cobs are doing

Two cobs, tilted seven degrees, each 6 × 12 units: a tapered body with a 3-unit kernel pattern, two husk leaves at the base, and a silk thread at the tip. At 260 px the pattern reads as kernels; at 64 px it reads as a gold sliver, which is what you want — no fake detail pretending to be legible.

Why twoOne cob is a garnish; two frame the state and make it a crest.
Why not biggerAny larger and they collide with the ring and squeeze the state below legibility.
Why tiltedVertical cobs look like a barcode. Seven degrees makes them feel placed.
Palette unchanged: highway #14432f · paper #f7f4ec · amber #e3a008 · ink #14150f. Preview only — nothing here is wired into lib/brand.mjs. If either version ships, the 32 px and 16 px fallbacks need a rule in the brand file.
