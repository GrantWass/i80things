State in the middle, cobs on both sides

Drawn as detailed vector cobs — husks, kernel rows, two-tone shading — not icons. The honest catch: that detail only exists above roughly 128 px. At 32 the cobs become amber commas, and at 16 the whole crest is a smudge. That does not kill the idea; it decides where it can be used.

Option A

The seal

Crest fills the badge, no type. State silhouette centred, cobs tilted outward like a seal. This is the version to use at 128 px and above — the Instagram avatar, the OG corner, anything printed.

Works at128 px and up. Perfect avatar, print, merch, a corner stamp on cards.
Fails at32 and below. The badge would have to fall back to the plain 80 there.
Reads asA state seal more than a logo — which suits a newsroom.

Option B

Seal with the sign

Same crest, but the interstate type stays at the top and the 80 comes back small at the bottom. More of a full logo, less of a stamp. Busier, and it starts to feel like a state agency badge.

Works at128 px. At 64 you have to drop the cobs anyway, so the crest stops being the crest.
Fails atEverything small. This is the mark that needs the most rules.
Reads asOfficial. Possibly too official — a state agency, not a newsroom.

Option C

In the band

The literal reading: state and two cobs squeezed into the strip above INTERSTATE, with the 80 untouched. Included because you asked, and because seeing it is faster than arguing about it.

Works atNowhere. The band is 10 units tall; the cobs are 5 units wide. There is no room to be realistic.
Fails atEvery size. At 260 px it looks like three stamps crammed into a strip.
ConclusionIf you want the crest, it replaces the 80 — it cannot live above it.
